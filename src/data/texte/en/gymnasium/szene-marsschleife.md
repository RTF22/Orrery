# Scene: Mars retrograde loop

[Mars](objekt:mars) usually moves across the starry sky from west to east. Around opposition, when
[Earth](objekt:earth) is between the [Sun](objekt:sun) and Mars, it turns around for a good two and
a half months: it becomes retrograde. This is an effect of perspective. Earth circles the Sun in one
year, Mars needs 687 days; on the inner track Earth overtakes it every 780 days, and while being
overtaken the slower vehicle seems to fall back against the background.

The scene puts the camera inside Earth (camera mode "From Earth") and keeps it looking at Mars; the
scale is then "Realistic" so that directions are right. From the set time it looks for the next
opposition and jumps to 81 days before it; with the clock in autumn 2026 that is the one of
19 February 2027. Then time runs at 2.7 days per second. A trail shows the last 365 days of its
path, plus the ecliptic, the apparent path of the Sun. Mars turns around in January 2027, runs back
about 19.5° until April, roughly 40 full-Moon widths, and then forward again. Because its orbit is
inclined by 1.85° to Earth's orbit, the path is a zigzag rather than a circle. Jupiter, which is
retrograde at about the same time, is in the picture too.

Ptolemy already described retrograde motion with epicycles, and Copernicus explained it as an
appearance caused by Earth's motion
([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](quelle:sep-copernicus)). More on the orbits:
[Orbital elements](thema:bahnelemente).

# Umbriel

Umbriel is the third of Uranus' five large moons by distance and, at 584.7 km in radius, only
marginally larger than [Ariel](objekt:ariel) – but geologically its opposite: with a geometric
albedo of 0.21, Umbriel is the darkest of the five, its surface old, densely cratered, and
showing no evidence of later resurfacing. Voyager 2 saw only Umbriel's sunlit southern hemisphere
during its single flyby on 24 January 1986 ([Smith et al. 1986](literatur:smith-1986)); more
recent insights come from Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope. This text presents
parameters, interior, surface, atmosphere and magnetosphere, and orbit, rotation and formation,
and finally describes what Orrery renders of it.

## Parameters and measurement

Unlike the slightly triaxial Miranda and Ariel, Umbriel appears spherical within measurement
accuracy in the Voyager 2 limb-coordinate surveys, as do Titania and Oberon
([Thomas 1988](literatur:thomas-1988)); a single radius value therefore suffices. As with
[Miranda](objekt:miranda) and [Ariel](objekt:ariel), Umbriel's mass parameter also comes from a
single flyby trajectory plus decades of ground-based astrometry
([Jacobson 2014](literatur:jacobson-2014)).

| Radius (limb coordinates/IAU) | $584.7\,\mathrm{km}$ | $2.8\,\mathrm{km}$ | limb coordinates | [JPL SSD satellite data](quelle:jpl-satelliten) |
| $\bar\rho$ | $1523\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | $41\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | from $GM$ and radius | [JPL SSD satellite data](quelle:jpl-satelliten) |
| Geometric albedo (visual) | $0.21$ | – | fact sheet | [Uranian Satellite Fact Sheet](quelle:nssdc-uranusmonde) |

With the catalog mass ($1.22 \cdot 10^{21}\,\mathrm{kg}$) and the radius of 584.7 km, the
resulting density is about $1.46\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$ (derivation) – exactly the NSSDC fact
sheet's value; the density computed from the table's $GM$ and radius above,
$1.52\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$, rests on a slightly different, more recent mass solution, as with
[Ariel](objekt:ariel) (1.54 versus 1.59 g/cm³). By catalog density, Umbriel is the lightest of
the four larger Uranian moons: Titania (1660 kg/m³), Oberon (1560) and Ariel (1590) are denser,
only Miranda (1200) is lighter. At a visual albedo of 0.21, Umbriel is also the darkest of the
five, a rank confirmed independently by HST photometry across several bands
([Karkoschka 2001](literatur:karkoschka-2001)). Because Voyager 2 saw only the southern
hemisphere, all surface values hold for a good half of the moon.

## Interior

In the same simplified two-component model of water ice ($917\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$) and
silicate rock ($3000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, as for [Miranda](objekt:miranda),
[Ariel](objekt:ariel) and [Iapetus](objekt:iapetus)), Umbriel's catalog density corresponds to a
rock mass fraction of about 54 % (derivation) – between Miranda's one third and Ariel's 60 %, but
without the tectonic or cryovolcanic surface signature that points to interior heat at Ariel (see
Surface). Interior models for all five large Uranian moons place Umbriel alongside Ariel, Titania
and Oberon as a possible host of a residual ocean, while Miranda is too small
([Castillo-Rogez et al. 2023](literatur:castillo-rogez-2023)); an independent heat budget finds
radiogenic heating alone insufficient for a long-lived ocean at Ariel and Umbriel, unlike the
larger Titania and Oberon, which could retain enough heat over billions of years
([Bierson and Nimmo 2022](literatur:bierson-2022)). A present-day ocean at Umbriel is therefore
considered possible by both works, but less certain than at Titania and Oberon. A direct test
would be measuring the secondary field induced by Uranus' time-varying magnetic field during a
flyby: models for all five large moons predict, given a sufficiently conductive ocean
($>2\,\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$, 0.4 to 6 km thick, even beneath an ice shell up to 50 km thick), a
signal exceeding the roughly 1 nT sensitivity typical of spacecraft magnetometers
([Weiss et al. 2021](literatur:weiss-2021)). Unlike Ariel, however, Umbriel's known surface
offers no tectonic structures from which elastic thicknesses and heat fluxes could be derived.

## Surface

By crater counting densities, Umbriel's surface is considered among the oldest in the Uranian
system, along with Miranda, Titania and Oberon and unlike the markedly younger Ariel
([Kirchoff et al. 2022](literatur:kirchoff-2022)); an earlier analysis of the same Voyager images,
based only on Umbriel, Titania and Oberon, had already reached the same conclusion
([Plescia 1987](literatur:plescia-1987)). A network of chasmata or smooth, subsequently infilled
floors as on Ariel is absent; apart from one striking feature, the known hemisphere shows little
evidence of later geological activity.

That feature is the 131 km diameter crater Wunda near the equator (about 8° south), on whose
floor Voyager 2 discovered a bright, ring-shaped deposit at least 10 km in radial width
([Smith et al. 1986](literatur:smith-1986)). Thermal and ballistic transport modeling shows that,
because of Uranus' extreme axial tilt (see Orbit, rotation and dynamics), carbon dioxide ice on
Umbriel migrates preferentially to low latitudes and accumulates there on geologically short
timescales; the crater's flat floor and central peak create an especially cold trap favorable to
CO2 ice inside Wunda, whose walls and peak explain the ring-shaped rather than area-filling
distribution ([Sori et al. 2017](literatur:sori-2017)). Whether the
bright ring could instead stem from impact-triggered cryovolcanism or from material vented from
an interior ocean is tested by a more recent modeling study, without disproving the pure CO2
cold-trap explanation ([Denton et al. 2026](literatur:denton-2026)).

Spectroscopically, Umbriel carries less CO2 ice than Ariel but more than Titania, while Oberon
shows none ([Cartwright et al. 2015](literatur:cartwright-2015)); as with Ariel and Titania, it
concentrates on the trailing hemisphere, while the leading hemisphere shows deeper water-ice
absorption bands ([Grundy et al. 2006](literatur:grundy-2006)). More recent JWST spectroscopy of
all four classical moons except Miranda instead favors a largely native origin for the carbon
oxides overall, brought along during accretion in the Uranian subnebula rather than arising
solely from radiation chemistry or interior venting, with only particle irradiation and seasonal
sublimation-condensation cycles shaping today's distribution
([Cartwright et al. 2026](literatur:cartwright-2026)) – a picture consistent with Wunda's
cold-trap mechanism, without fully replacing the earlier, radiolytic interpretation
([Cartwright et al. 2015](literatur:cartwright-2015)).

Why Umbriel is so much darker overall than its neighbors remains open (see Open questions). Two
positions stand opposed: the old, barely renewed surface could have accumulated dark, carbon-rich
weathering material over billions of years that was buried on the more active moons; independently,
hemispheric color and brightness asymmetries between the leading and trailing hemispheres of the
classical Uranian moons increase with distance from Uranus
([Buratti and Mosher 1991](literatur:buratti-1991)) – a hint of externally delivered dark dust from
the irregular moons, similar to Saturn's [Iapetus](objekt:iapetus)
([Cartwright et al. 2018](literatur:cartwright-2018)).

## Atmosphere and magnetosphere

Umbriel carries no atmosphere; as with Ariel, its mass and surface temperature are insufficient
to bind CO2 permanently in gaseous form. The moon lies within Uranus' magnetosphere, whose
charged particles continually irradiate its surface – a factor both for the radiation weathering
that could contribute to the surface's darkness (see Surface) and for at least one of the three
competing interpretations of the CO2 concentrated in Wunda (see above and Open questions).

## Orbit, rotation and dynamics

At $i = 179.944^\circ$, Umbriel's osculating orbit in the model lies only $0.056^\circ$ from the
degeneracy at $180^\circ$ – markedly closer to Uranus' equatorial plane than Miranda's 4.34°,
though not quite as close as Ariel's 0.003°; the NSSDC fact sheet instead gives an orbital
inclination of 0.13°, a small, unresolved difference of 0.07° between the sources (convention as
under [Orbital Elements](thema:bahnelemente)). Like all 21 moons captured in the model, Umbriel
rotates synchronously, with a residual deviation between rotation and orbital period of only
$0.020 \cdot 10^{-6}$ relative; the direction toward Uranus therefore drifts from 0.8° at the
epoch to 1.2° a century later – one of the smallest librations of the five (figures as in
[Synchronous Rotation](thema:gebundene-rotation)).

Umbriel no longer sits in a mean-motion resonance today, but was a partner in both resonances of
the system's classical orbital dynamics: first a 3:1 resonance with [Miranda](objekt:miranda),
thought to explain Miranda's present inclination and from whose required tidal damping Uranus'
dissipation factor $Q$ can be bounded below 39,000
([Tittemore and Wisdom 1989](literatur:tittemore-1989)), then – after Miranda and
[Ariel](objekt:ariel) passed through a 5:3 resonance – a 2:1 resonance with Ariel itself, during
which the eccentricities of both moons could fluctuate considerably
([Tittemore and Wisdom 1990](literatur:tittemore-1990); basic concepts under
[Orbital Resonances](thema:resonanzen)). A newer modeling effort spanning the whole system instead
places the youngest and most consequential resonance directly between Ariel and Umbriel: a 5:3
passage during which the inclinations of all five moons were raised, before subsequent secular
resonances restored Ariel's and Umbriel's present, nearly vanishing inclinations
([Ćuk et al. 2020](literatur:cuk-2020)) – which of the two pictures is correct remains undecided
(see Open questions). Under either reading, Umbriel was involved in resonance passages twice.

## Formation and evolution

Like the other regular moons of the giant planets, Umbriel most likely formed in a debris disk
around Uranus, whether after a giant impact that could also explain the planet's extreme axial
tilt, or through orderly co-accretion with the planet itself (figures and discussion under
[Uranus](objekt:uranus) and [Axial Tilt](thema:achsneigung)). The resonance passages described
above are considered the central driver that heated orbits system-wide and released the moons
into their present, non-resonant configuration; different approaches to the required tidal
damping in Uranus arrive at dissipation factors between roughly 15,000 and 39,000
([Tittemore and Wisdom 1989](literatur:tittemore-1989); [Ćuk et al. 2020](literatur:cuk-2020)) –
why the same passages left Ariel young and active but Umbriel old and unchanged remains open (see
Open questions). Uranus and its moon system formed within the broader formation history of the
outer solar system, in which Neptune also migrated outward (context under
[Formation of the Solar System](thema:entstehung)).

## Open questions

- **Why is Umbriel so much darker and geologically less altered than Ariel?** Both moons are
  similar in size and involved in the same resonance passages; whether age and lack of renewal
  alone suffice, or whether exogenous dark dust also plays a role, is not settled
  ([Cartwright et al. 2018](literatur:cartwright-2018);
  [Buratti and Mosher 1991](literatur:buratti-1991)).
- **Where does the CO2 in Wunda and on Umbriel generally come from?** Radiation chemistry acting
  on irradiated water ice ([Cartwright et al. 2015](literatur:cartwright-2015)), a possible
  interior origin vented through cryovolcanism ([Denton et al. 2026](literatur:denton-2026)), and
  a largely primordial inventory brought along at accretion
  ([Cartwright et al. 2026](literatur:cartwright-2026)) stand as competing explanations.
- **Does Umbriel host a present-day ocean?** Interior models consider it possible but less certain
  than at Titania and Oberon ([Castillo-Rogez et al. 2023](literatur:castillo-rogez-2023);
  [Bierson and Nimmo 2022](literatur:bierson-2022)); a magnetic-field measurement during a future
  flyby is considered the most promising direct test
  ([Weiss et al. 2021](literatur:weiss-2021)).
- **Northern hemisphere unknown.** Voyager 2 saw only the sunlit southern hemisphere in 1986; no
  close-up images exist of the composition, crater density, or possible further bright deposits
  of the northern hemisphere.

## In the model

- **Orbital reference:** Like the other four large Uranian moons, Umbriel orbits with reference
  frame `parentEquator` on [Uranus'](objekt:uranus) equatorial plane, using osculating Horizons
  elements at epoch J2000 ($a = 266012.19\,\mathrm{km}$, $e = 0.00417$, $i = 179.944^\circ$). The
  resulting distance from the degeneracy at $180^\circ$ (0.056°) differs from the NSSDC fact
  sheet's value (0.13°) – a small, unresolved difference between the two sources, as with Miranda
  (0.09°, see [Miranda](objekt:miranda)).
- **Node rate:** `nodeDot` is zero – as with the other three larger Uranian moons, a case of
  numerical degeneracy of the node at $i \approx 180^\circ$. Horizons' own osculating node,
  however, wanders more evenly here than for Ariel or Titania: measured at 3.7° per year (figures
  as in [Orbital Elements](thema:bahnelemente)) against 2.8° per year from the nodal period
  $P_\mathrm{node} = 129.745\,\mathrm{a}$ of the JPL table
  ($360/129.745 = 2.7747^\circ$/year, derivation). Against the fixture `monde-horizons.json`, the
  position with `nodeDot = 0` still stays close to the reference: 41 to 1158 km at the five
  reference dates (at most 0.070 % of the orbital circumference), well below Ariel's maximum of
  2378 km (derivation).
- **Apsidal rate:** `lpDot` ($283.9274^\circ$ per century) follows the two-term project formula
  $(360/P_\mathrm{apsis} - 360/P_\mathrm{node}) \cdot 100$ with the JPL table values
  $P_\mathrm{apsis} = 64.126\,\mathrm{a}$ and $P_\mathrm{node} = 129.745\,\mathrm{a}$:
  $(360/64.126 - 360/129.745) \cdot 100 = 283.9274$ (the derivation matches the dataset value
  exactly) – the same table column that sets `nodeDot` to zero for an entirely different,
  empirical reason.
- **Orbital period:** From `LDot`, $360^\circ/\dot L = 4.144177\,\mathrm{d}$; the data panel shows
  the Kepler orbital period computed from semi-major axis and masses, about $4.14502\,\mathrm{d}$,
  some 0.02 % longer. `rotationPeriodH` ($-99.46025\,\mathrm{h}$) matches
  $360^\circ/\dot L \cdot 24$ practically exactly – the synchronous rotation from Orbit, rotation
  and dynamics above.
- **Pole:** $257.43^\circ / -15.10^\circ$ is fixed in the dataset and – as with Ariel, Titania and
  Oberon – a simplification; only Miranda carries a slightly different declination. The data
  panel's axial tilt (`achsneigungDeg`, the angle between this pole and the orbital normal formed
  from position and velocity at the epoch, flipped for a negative rotation period) is
  $0.182^\circ$ (derivation) – close to Ariel's $0.140^\circ$ and well below Miranda's $4.334^\circ$.
- **Texture:** Umbriel carries no texture, but a fallback color approximating the fact-sheet
  albedo: as with the other four large Uranian moons, the only available USGS/JPL map mosaic
  consists to a substantial extent of unlit area because of Voyager's one-sided illumination
  (`ASSETS.md`); Wunda itself is not rendered as a distinct feature.
- **Shadows:** Umbriel is among the up to four simultaneously drawn sphere occluders
  (`MAX_OKKLUDER`) when it has the largest rendered angular radius among the Uranian moons; among
  the five large moons, Oberon, as the smallest, always drops out (figures as in
  [Eclipses](thema:finsternis)).
- **Scale:** Umbriel is a satellite (`isSatellite`, its `parent` is Uranus, not the Sun); its
  orbital radius therefore scales with `sizeScale` like its own and Uranus' radius, not with the
  distance compression applied to planetary orbits. Further simplifications:
  [Limits of the model](thema:modell).

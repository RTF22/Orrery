# Mimas

Mimas is the innermost and, at 198.5 km in radius, the smallest of Saturn's seven large moons: an
ancient, densely cratered ice body whose Herschel crater measures about a third of Mimas's own
diameter and has earned it the nickname "Death Star". Cassini images show a libration in Mimas
twice as large as expected for a fully frozen, hydrostatic body – a finding that has left Mimas
suspended, since 2014, between a strongly non-hydrostatic rocky core and its own young ocean. This
text presents parameters and measurement, interior, surface, atmosphere and magnetosphere, and
orbit, rotation and formation, and finally describes what Orrery renders of it.

## Parameters and measurement

Cassini's radio tracking (ephemeris SAT441) yields Mimas's mass parameter directly from the
Doppler shift of the spacecraft's radio signal; combined with the volume derived from Cassini
images, this gives a mean density of about 1150 kg m⁻³ – barely more than that of water ice
(917 kg m⁻³) and well below that of homogeneous silicate-ice mixtures, pointing to internal
porosity or a small rock fraction ([JPL SSD satellite data](quelle:jpl-satelliten)). Mimas is also
markedly triaxial: 208 × 197 × 191 km, derived from a photogrammetric control-point network built
from Cassini images of the nominal mission ([Thomas 2010](literatur:thomas-2010)); the
volume-equivalent spherical radius from these three semi-axes is 198.5 km – the value the catalog
also carries.

At around 1150 kg m⁻³, Mimas is among the least dense of the mid-sized Saturn moons.

## Interior

The 2014 analysis of seven years of Cassini images found a forced libration amplitude for Mimas of
$0.838^\circ \pm 0.017^\circ$ (2σ) – roughly twice as large as expected for a fully frozen,
homogeneous body in hydrostatic equilibrium. Two interpretations are consistent with the same
measurement: either a strongly non-hydrostatic, elongated rock-ice interior, or a hydrostatic body
with a global ocean beneath an ice shell 24 to 31 km thick; the libration measurement alone does
not separate the two readings ([Tajeddine et al. 2014](literatur:tajeddine-2014)). As described for
[Enceladus](objekt:enceladus), Mimas's interior remains similarly uncertain – a finding that shows
how hard it stays to distinguish a porous core from an ocean among the mid-sized Saturn moons in
general.

A 2024 analysis of Mimas's orbital motion – libration phase together with pericentre precession –
favours the ocean reading: it yields an ice shell 20 to 30 km thick over an ocean that appears to
have formed only 5 to 15 million years ago, triggered by a – since decaying – tripling of Mimas's
orbital eccentricity under the gravitational influence of other Saturn moons
([Lainey et al. 2024](literatur:lainey-2024)). If correct, Mimas's ocean would still be at the very
start of its evolution.

Why Enceladus, with its ocean, is geologically active while Mimas, despite roughly forty times
stronger tidal dissipation per unit mass assuming the same interior structure, appears essentially
inert – the "Mimas paradox" – remains open: proposals range from a chemical trigger during core
hydration that only Enceladus's higher rock fraction supplies, to a simply younger Mimas whose
ocean has not yet existed long enough to reshape the surface
([Ćuk et al. 2024](literatur:cuk-2024)). More on the core and moment-of-inertia factor for moons in
general under [Interior structure](thema:innerer-aufbau).

## Surface

Mimas's most striking feature is the Herschel crater: about 140 km in diameter, roughly a third of
Mimas's own diameter (397 km), with a central peak about 6 km high and a floor lying about 11 km
below the surrounding terrain ([Blanco-Rojas and Sori 2025](literatur:blanco-rojas-2025), after
Moore et al. 2004). Voyager 1 discovered the crater during its Saturn flyby in November 1980
([Smith et al. 1981](literatur:smith-1981)); the resemblance widely noted since then to the
fictional space station from a well-known science-fiction film series gave Mimas its "Death Star"
nickname. An impact of this size should have come close to breaking the moon apart (see Open
questions); simulations of the impact mechanics only find a solution consistent with Herschel's
shape if the base of the ice shell already lay near the melting point – either as a considerably
thicker shell over an ocean or, as a 2025 extension of the same model series shows, even within a
fully frozen ice shell with a warm base
([Denton and Rhoden 2022](literatur:denton-2022); [Denton and Rhoden 2025](literatur:denton-2025)).

Away from Herschel, Mimas shows an old landscape densely covered in smaller craters, typical of the
inner Saturn moons. A V-shaped temperature anomaly on the leading hemisphere, centred near 0°
latitude and 180° longitude and nicknamed "Pac-Man" for its shape, runs up to 15 K warmer at night
and cooler by day than its surroundings – a sign of markedly higher thermal inertia
($66 \pm 23$ versus under $16\,\mathrm{J}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\,\mathrm{K}^{-1}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1/2}$)
inside the anomaly. The proposed cause is a compaction of the uppermost regolith by
high-energy electrons from Saturn's magnetosphere, which preferentially strike the leading side
([Howett et al. 2011a](literatur:howett-2011a)).

## Atmosphere and magnetosphere

Mimas carries no atmosphere of its own; its small mass and low surface temperature rule out a
bound gas envelope. The moon lies deep within Saturn's magnetosphere and E ring, where charged
particles continually irradiate its surface – the "Pac-Man" anomaly is the clearest visible trace
of this interaction ([Howett et al. 2011a](literatur:howett-2011a); see Surface). Unlike the active
[Enceladus](objekt:enceladus), Mimas does not feed any ring material of its own; its contribution
to the system stays purely gravitational (see Orbit, rotation and dynamics).

## Orbit, rotation and dynamics

Mimas orbits Saturn in 0.942 days (figures as in
[Saturn's rings edge-on](szene:saturn-ringkante)), on an orbit unusually eccentric for a large moon
($e \approx 0.02$), and rotates in a bound state. With [Tethys](objekt:tethys), Mimas is locked in
a second-order 4:2 mean-motion resonance: the angle
$2\lambda_\mathrm{Mi} - 4\lambda_\mathrm{Te} + \Omega_\mathrm{Mi} + \Omega_\mathrm{Te}$ librates
around 0° with an amplitude of 95° and a period of 70 years; because Tethys is about sixteen times
more massive than Mimas, the resonance raises essentially only Mimas's orbital inclination, not
that of Tethys ([Ćuk et al. 2024](literatur:cuk-2024); derivation alongside every orbital resonance
in the Solar System under [Orbital resonances](thema:resonanzen)). Mimas's own eccentricity is
unaffected by this resonance and must predate it; possible origins include earlier, now inactive
resonances with [Dione](objekt:dione) (3:1) or Enceladus (3:2), though exactly how either played
out depends on hard-to-pin-down three-body effects involving Titan and remains open
([Ćuk et al. 2024](literatur:cuk-2024)).

The same 2:1 resonance that underlies Mimas's present 4:2 coupling with Tethys coincides spatially
with the outer edge of Saturn's B ring and the inner edge of the Cassini Division: there it clears
the ring material almost completely and produces the sharpest edge in the whole ring system
([Esposito 2010](literatur:esposito-2010); [Cuzzi et al. 2010](literatur:cuzzi-2010);
classification under [Ring systems](thema:ringe)). Mimas itself orbits well inside the Roche limit
of about 129,000 km that applies to compact ice – that it nonetheless persists there as a solid
body sets it apart from the far smaller, loosely bound ring particles and ring moons in its
neighbourhood.

## Formation and evolution

How old Mimas is ties closely to the question of its ocean. If it formed, like the other mid-sized
Saturn moons, out of the Saturn disc shortly after the planet itself, the Herschel crater would
have to be an event several billion years old on a body that has stayed cold and unchanged ever
since. If Mimas instead formed comparatively recently – favoured by a fast orbital evolution
dominated by the nearby rings – a young, still-evolving ocean would also be more plausible; there
is, however, no independent evidence for an age postdating the rings themselves
([Ćuk et al. 2024](literatur:cuk-2024); classification alongside the other origin hypotheses for
the Saturn moons under [Formation of the Solar System](thema:entstehung)).

How the nearly moon-destroying Herschel impact fits into this picture is likewise open: models of
the impact mechanics find a solution consistent with Herschel's shape either with a considerably
thicker, ocean-bearing ice shell than today's or – per a 2025 extension of the same work – with a
fully frozen ice shell whose base already lay near the melting point; either way, Mimas's interior
was warmer at the time of impact than it is today, regardless of how old the moon is overall
([Denton and Rhoden 2022](literatur:denton-2022); [Denton and Rhoden 2025](literatur:denton-2025)).

## Open questions

- **Ocean or strongly non-hydrostatic interior?** The measured libration amplitude is consistent
  with both readings; Tajeddine et al. themselves leave the question explicitly open
  ([Tajeddine et al. 2014](literatur:tajeddine-2014)). A more recent analysis of orbital motion and
  pericentre precession favours a young ocean
  ([Lainey et al. 2024](literatur:lainey-2024)), yet neither the absence of surface signs of active
  tidal flexing nor Mimas's still-undamped eccentricity is fully explained by it.
- **How did Mimas survive the Herschel impact?** Simulations succeed with a considerably thicker,
  ocean-bearing ice shell as well as with a fully frozen ice shell whose base already lay near the
  melting point; both cases require a warmer interior at the time of impact, and which of the two
  applies remains open
  ([Denton and Rhoden 2022](literatur:denton-2022); [Denton and Rhoden 2025](literatur:denton-2025)).
- **Cause and age of the "Pac-Man" temperature anomaly.** Attributing it to high-energy electron
  bombardment explains its location on the leading side, but not why the boundary is so sharp or
  how long the pattern has already persisted
  ([Howett et al. 2011a](literatur:howett-2011a)).
- **Is the unrelaxed Herschel topography consistent with a young ocean?** Models of viscous crater
  relaxation find that even an ice shell only 15 km thick flattens the crater by just 2 to 9 % over
  25 million years – too little to rule out the ocean hypothesis using the observed, barely
  relaxed shape, contrary to the common assumption that ocean worlds must show relaxed surfaces
  ([Blanco-Rojas and Sori 2025](literatur:blanco-rojas-2025)).

## In the model

- **Orbital reference:** Like the other Saturn moons, Mimas moves in the `parentEquator` reference
  on [Saturn's](objekt:saturn) equatorial plane, with osculating Horizons elements at epoch J2000
  ($a = 186036.82\,\mathrm{km}$, $e = 0.02176$) – figures and convention as under
  [Orbital elements](thema:bahnelemente).
- **Node and apsidal precession:** `nodeDot` ($-36511.1562^\circ$ per century) gives a retrograde
  nodal period of 0.986 years. `lp` carries the standard longitude of pericentre ($\Omega +
  \omega$); its rate `lpDot` ($+36511.1562^\circ$ per century, equal in magnitude but opposite in
  sign to `nodeDot`) shows that Mimas's argument of pericentre $\omega$ itself precesses at
  $73022.3^\circ$ per century, twice as fast as the node, completing one cycle in just 0.493 years
  (worked out from the code).
- **Orbital period:** From `LDot` follows $360^\circ/\dot L = 0.9424$ days, adopted as in
  [Saturn's rings edge-on](szene:saturn-ringkante). The data panel instead shows the Kepler orbital
  period computed from the semi-major axis and the masses (`umlaufzeitTage`), $0.9475$ days, about
  0.54 % longer – figures as in [Bound rotation](thema:gebundene-rotation). `rotationPeriodH`
  (22.61813 h) matches $360^\circ/\dot L$ to $0.088 \cdot 10^{-6}$ relative; the angle between the
  map centre and the direction to Saturn stands at $143.6^\circ$ at epoch and at $145.7^\circ$
  after a hundred years (the same figures as there).
- **Fixture finding, not a faulty position:** `monde.fixture.test.ts` allows Mimas, at one of the
  five test dates (2026), a position deviation of up to 20 % of the orbital circumference instead
  of the 5 % that applies to every other Saturn moon (`POSITIONS_SCHRANKE_ANTEIL`). The header
  comment in `saturn-monde.ts` classifies this not as a data error but as an example of how a
  linearly propagated orbital element (`LDot` held constant) cannot, in principle, reproduce the
  decades-long 4:2 libration described above: the deviation does not grow or shrink monotonically
  with time from J2000 but tracks the libration phase.
- **Pole:** $40.66^\circ / 83.52^\circ$ from the IAU rotation elements – identical to the data sets
  for [Enceladus](objekt:enceladus), Tethys and Dione, because the underlying SPICE kernel gives no
  individual precession for any of the four, only a shared pole fixed at epoch
  ([Archinal et al. 2018](literatur:archinal-2018)).
- **Albedo and texture:** The catalog albedo of 0.6 is the fact sheet's geometric albedo; the
  texture is a seamless Cassini mosaic with no unlit areas, unlike several Uranus moons
  (`ASSETS.md`).
- **Shadow:** `waehleOkkluder` picks Saturn's four moons with the largest rendered angular radius
  as spherical occluders: Titan, Tethys, Dione and Rhea; Mimas never casts a shadow on Saturn in
  the model, even though its real shadow window of $18.2^\circ$ – the widest of any Saturn moon –
  would, at Saturn's $26.7^\circ$ axial tilt, be open more often than any other's (figures as in
  [Eclipses](thema:finsternis) and the Saturn text). Seen from Mimas itself, though, Saturn
  is always available as an occluder: Mimas is very much shadowed, it just never casts one itself.
- **Ring resonance not modelled:** Saturn's ring in the model is a single, static textured disc
  with no density profile (`render/rings.ts`); Mimas's real role as the resonance source for the B
  ring's outer edge (see Orbit, rotation and dynamics) has no counterpart there.
- **Scale:** Mimas is a satellite (`isSatellite`, its `parent` is Saturn, not the Sun); its orbital
  radius around Saturn therefore scales, like its own and Saturn's radius, with `sizeScale`, not
  with the distance compression applied to the planetary orbits.
- **Data panel:** The mass carried in the data set ($3.79 \cdot 10^{19}\,\mathrm{kg}$) gives, times
  $G$ per CODATA 2018, a $GM$ of $2.5296\,\mathrm{km^3\,s^{-2}}$, about 10,413 ppm (1.04 %) above
  the value measured by radio tracking in the table above – a markedly larger deviation than for
  the other Saturn moons in this stage. Three-figure rounding alone does not explain it: the
  measured $GM$ implies a mass of $3.751 \cdot 10^{19}\,\mathrm{kg}$, which rounds to
  $0.375 \cdot 10^{20}\,\mathrm{kg}$, not the fact sheet's $0.379 \cdot 10^{20}\,\mathrm{kg}$; the
  fact sheet evidently carries an older mass determination for Mimas that differs from the newer
  SAT441 solution (Jacobson 2022) for reasons beyond rounding. Further simplifications:
  [Limits of the model](thema:modell).
